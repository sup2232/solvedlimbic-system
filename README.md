Abstract
We propose SYSTEM 5.0, a comprehensive, physically grounded framework unifying
neuroaesthetics, predictive coding, and cultural evolution. We formalize artistic value across
three axes (Technique, Expressivity, Innovation) and four dimensions of verisimilitude
(Biological, Spectral, Technical, Informational). Building on this, we introduce the theory of
informational telepathy: the capacity of the human limbic system to predict intentions via
the decoding of compressed symbolic information load. We model collective dynamics using
symbolic frequency and limbic synchrony, explaining phenomena ranging from orchestral
coordination to shared altered states of consciousness (e.g., dreams, near-death
experiences, psychedelic visions) as instances of limbic resonance. Furthermore, we
formalize the symbolic decoding gap to explain cultural shock in isolated populations.
All mechanisms are falsifiable, with operational metrics provided.

Introduction
Formal theories of art, communication, and cultural evolution face a persistent tension
between subjective phenomenological experience and measurable biological structure.
Classical accounts often reduce art to resemblance or technical skill, while computational
approaches attempt to quantify aesthetic value through image statistics or algorithmic
complexity. Concurrently, human communication is frequently described metaphorically as
"reading minds," yet no physical mechanism has been formally proposed to explain how
static symbols enable one individual to predict the future actions of another with high
accuracy without direct neural coupling.
SYSTEM 5.0 resolves these disparate threads into a unified neuro-semiotic framework. We
propose that aesthetic evaluation, interpersonal prediction, and collective cultural states are
all governed by the limbic system's capacity for predictive decoding of compressed symbolic
information.
1.1 Core Hypotheses
H₁ (Multidimensional Art & Absurdity): Artistic value is a three-dimensional vector
(Technique, Expressivity, Innovation) evaluated against four types of verisimilitude.
"Perfection" is an operational threshold that triggers a measurable neurocognitive
response of absurdity.
H₂ (Informational Telepathy): Symbols carry compressed informational load.
High-load symbols activate hippocampal-amygdalar-prefrontal circuits, generating
probabilistic behavioral models of the symbol's producer—a physical, biological form
of telepathy.
H₃ (Cultural Shock & The Decoding Gap): Difficulties in cultural assimilation (e.g.,
isolated populations) are not cognitive deficits but predictive collapses caused by an
exponentially widening symbolic decoding gap.
H₄ (Limbic Resonance & Altered States): Collective synchrony and shared altered
states (dreams, NDEs, psychedelics) are manifestations of limbic resonance, occurring
when external symbolic frequency drops, unmasking universal spectral archetypes.

The Neuro-Semiotic Foundations of Art and
Verisimilitude
2.1 The Three Axes of Artistic Value
Let W denote an artwork or symbolic artifact. We represent its artistic profile as a vector:
A( W ) = ( T( W ), E( W ), I( W ) ) ∈ [0, 10]³
where T is Technique (mastery of medium), E is Expressivity (capacity to evoke limbic
affect), and I is Innovation (departure from established conventions).
2.2 The Four Dimensions of Verisimilitude
Verisimilitude is decomposed into four components operating on distinct temporal scales:
Biological (VB): Similarity to human morphology (Timescale: 500–1,000 years).
Spectral (VS): Activation of archetypal, culturally stabilized visual patterns (Timescale:
50–100 years).
Technical (VT): Fidelity to physical reality via technological mediation (Timescale: 3–8
years).
Informational (VI): The density of compressed symbolic data activating limbic
predictions (Timescale: 1–5 years).
2.3 Perfection and the Neurocognitive Response of Absurdity
An artwork W achieves operational perfection if:
min{ T, E, I } ≥ 9.5 ∧ min{ VB, VS, VT, VI } ≥ 0.95 ∧ Q( W ) ≥ 9.0
where Q( W ) is a composite quality score (effort, iteration, peer feedback, originality).
Perfection triggers Absurdity (A), a measurable response combining cognitive surprise and
limbic reward:
A( W ) = ( ΔNAcc( W ), τfix( W ), Rverb( W ) )
where ΔNAcc is the relative increase in nucleus accumbens activation (fMRI), τfix is visual
fixation duration (eye-tracking), and Rverb is the subjective report of awe or impossibility.

Informational Telepathy: Limbic Prediction via
Symbolic Load
3.1 Informational Load
We define the informational loadL( s ) of a symbol s as the total number of
emotional-predictive bits activated in the limbic system:
L( s ) = Lsemantic( s ) + Lemotional( s ) + Lpredictive( s ) + Lcontextual( s )
While an isolated letter carries 0–10 bits, a modern technical symbol (e.g., a legal contract or
UI interface) carries 10⁵–10⁶ bits, requiring years of limbic calibration to decode.
3.2 The Four-Node Limbic Decoding Circuit
Informational telepathy operates via a specific neural circuit:
Hippocampus (Context Retrieval): Generates a contextual prior P( context | s ).
Amygdala (Valence Assignment): Assigns emotional valence v( s ) ∈ [-1, 1] and
arousal a( s ) ∈ [0, 1].
Mirror Neuron System (Motor Simulation): Generates an embodied prediction of
implied actions.
Prefrontal Cortex (Integration): Outputs a coherent predictive model â of the
producer's next action a.
The formal prediction model is:
â = arg max a P( a | s, context, v( s ), a( s ) )
Telepathic Accuracy is defined as 1 - H( â | a ), where H is the conditional entropy between
predicted and actual actions.

Collective Dynamics: Frequency, Synchrony,
and Resonance
4.1 Symbolic Frequency and Limbic Synchrony
Let fsym be the symbolic frequency (rate of introduction of new symbols into an ecosystem).
Let Σ ∈ [0, 1] be limbic synchrony (the degree to which a group decodes symbols with
identical predictive models).
Highly synchronized groups (e.g., orchestras, elite dance troupes) exhibit Informational
Telepathy in High Synchrony. The "sheet music" or "choreography" serves as a shared
symbolic alphabet. Musicians do not merely react; they predict the actions of their peers via
mirror-neuron simulation, achieving Σ → 1.
4.2 Limbic Resonance in Altered States of Consciousness
SYSTEM 5.0 provides a non-mystical explanation for shared experiences in altered states
(dreams, near-death experiences [NDEs], and psychedelic visions).
Under normal waking consciousness, high external fsym dominates limbic processing. In
altered states (e.g., via 5-HT2A agonists like psilocybin, or hypoxia in NDEs), external fsym
→ 0. The cortical filter is inhibited, and the limbic system defaults to processing innate,
high-load Spectral Archetypes (e.g., tunnels, light, geometric entities, parental figures).
Because the human limbic hardware and spectral archetypes are universally shared,
individuals in altered states experience Limbic Resonance—they report identical "realms"
(e.g., heaven, hell, machine-elf encounters) not because these places exist externally, but
because the limbic system is decompressing the same universal biological archive in the
absence of external symbolic noise.

Cultural Evolution and Shock
5.1 The Symbolic Decoding Gap
Let SM be the modern symbolic ecosystem and SI be that of an isolated population. The
symbolic decoding gap is:
Δsym = L( SM ) - L( SI ∩ SM )
As technological complexity grows, Δsym → ∞. When an isolated individual encounters
modern symbols, the hippocampus retrieves no context (P ≈ 0), and the amygdala defaults
to threat (v < 0, a → 1). This results in Predictive Collapse—manifesting as cultural shock,
anxiety, and withdrawal. This is a decoding failure, not a cognitive deficit.
5.2 Informational Colonialism
Because modern individuals share basal biological/spectral verisimilitude with isolated
populations, they can partially predict isolated behaviors. The reverse is impossible. This
Asymmetric Telepathy maps directly onto colonial power structures, allowing dominant
groups to manipulate or marginalize isolated groups via indecipherable symbolic systems
(e.g., legal codes, bureaucratic interfaces).
